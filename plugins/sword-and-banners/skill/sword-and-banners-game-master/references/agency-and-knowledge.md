# Agency and Knowledge

Use this reference when player choice, consent, authority, information access, recognition, allegiance, surrender, lethal intent, or NPC independence could be ambiguous.

## Tang Wei's protected agency

Never invent a consequential voluntary choice for Wei. Protected decisions include:
- spoken dialogue, formal answers, testimony, promises, oaths, petitions, and confessions;
- private thoughts, beliefs, attraction, loyalty, emotional conclusions, or motives;
- allegiance, betrayal, defection, surrender, mercy, execution, and lethal intent;
- gifts, voluntary spending, transfers, loans, contracts, purchases, wagers, and bribes;
- courtship, marriage, divorce, adoption, inheritance choices, household decisions, and other family commitments;
- acceptance or refusal of office, patronage, command, strategic duty, or permanent doctrine;
- irreversible treatment, mutilation, permanent equipment sacrifice, or comparable body decisions;
- travel destination when no destination has been chosen.

The runtime may establish involuntary consequences such as being wounded, arrested, delayed, surprised, refused, outmaneuvered, dismissed, or physically moved by force. Narrate those when committed.

## Declared intent can carry through obvious execution

Do not ask for a new choice at every mechanical substep. If Wei clearly chooses to attend a known appointment at a known location, ordinary departure, travel, arrival, and presentation may be sequenced when the runtime supports them and no new tradeoff appears.

Stop when execution introduces a genuinely new choice such as:
- different routes with meaningful danger or time cost;
- unexpected political conditions;
- payment or contractual commitment;
- conflicting orders;
- a new target;
- lethal versus nonlethal action;
- abandoning another obligation;
- committing reserves or another strategic asset.

## Standing authority and delegation

Saved delegation, orders, offices, military command, House roles, and institutional authority may cause NPCs or organizations to act without asking Wei every time. That is not a violation of player agency when the action remains within persisted scope.

Never expand a standing order beyond its saved meaning merely because expansion would be convenient.

## NPC independence

NPCs are not extensions of the player. They may:
- disagree;
- recommend a different course;
- refuse when they have lawful or personal grounds;
- negotiate;
- conceal information they are entitled and motivated to conceal;
- misunderstand;
- act under their own office or standing orders;
- pursue family, House, state, economic, or military goals;
- leave, delay, interrupt, petition, report, or escalate through institutions.

Their behavior should arise from saved knowledge, authority, relationships, reputation access, temperament evidence, resources, doctrine, obligations, injuries, incentives, and present risk.

Do not make an NPC hostile merely to create drama. Do not make an NPC compliant merely to reward the player.

## Organizational agency

States, Houses, armies, institutions, mercenary companies, markets, factions, and households can act independently when the runtime's causal hosts and rules allow it.

The world does not wait for Wei between scenes. Qin may appoint officers, Zhao may reinforce a frontier, a House may pursue a marriage, a market may tighten terms, a commander may train troops, and an institution may continue work without Wei initiating each event.

Player importance does not imply world centrality.

## Knowledge classes

Keep these categories distinct:

**Observation**: Wei directly perceives it now.

**Memory**: Wei previously perceived or learned it and can reasonably recall it.

**Verified report**: a source or institution supplied information with enough provenance to treat it as established from Wei's perspective.

**Estimate**: a bounded judgment derived from evidence, such as troop count from dust, camp size, scout observation, or logistical footprint.

**Inference**: a conclusion Wei can reasonably draw from observable facts and training.

**Rumor**: information circulating without reliable verification.

**Restricted knowledge**: true campaign information not available to Wei.

Never convert restricted knowledge into an inference by revealing the answer in disguised language.

## Information provenance

When information changes a decision, make its source visible when useful:
- Wei saw it;
- a named scout reported it;
- a clerk showed a sealed record;
- a merchant heard it on a route;
- a prisoner claimed it;
- an officer estimated it;
- a formal dispatch verified it.

Confidence and source quality can matter even when exact numeric confidence remains hidden from normal prose.

## Recognition and identity

A stored person record does not mean Wei recognizes the person on sight. Recognition should depend on player-visible prior contact, description, heraldry, introduction, office, uniform, reputation, or another lawful cue.

Recognition is reciprocal but not symmetrical. An NPC may lawfully know Tang Wei or House Tang through a saved institutional audience even when no personal relationship edge exists. When a present scene-cast row exposes `player_identity_awareness`, let that NPC recognize only the listed public fact classes. Do not convert institutional awareness into friendship, trust, loyalty, access, consent, secret knowledge, exact confidential force strength, or private stats. Absence of the cue does not override separately established direct personal knowledge.

Unknown identities stay unknown until revealed through valid play.

## Historical knowledge

External historical knowledge, model memory, wiki knowledge, and canonical future events are not player knowledge and do not dictate future outcomes.

Use historical context only where the game data establishes background conditions, institutions, past events, or pressures. Future history remains contingent on simulation unless the runtime explicitly commits an event.

## Clarify only consequential ambiguity

Do not interrogate the player about harmless details. Clarify when ambiguity would choose a protected decision or cause a materially different persistent write.

Examples that require clarification:
- `Deal with the prisoner` when mercy, execution, release, or custody differ materially.
- `Handle the offer` when accepting a contract would spend money or create obligations.
- `Take them out` when lethal versus nonlethal intent matters.

Examples usually not requiring clarification:
- ordinary walking within a chosen route;
- routine greetings that carry no consequential promise, if dialogue itself is not required to resolve mechanics;
- unimportant hand placement or posture.
