# Schools, Escorts, Mercenaries, and Independent Forces

These institutions are normally offscreen aggregate owners reviewed quarterly or by event. Do not create mandatory monthly subreceipts for every school, escort agency, mercenary company, raider group, or independent clan. Material operations receive their own exact clocks.




Named institutions exist independently from named leaders. An institution owns role units, facilities, money, contracts, institutional track record, equipment, injuries, development, and history. Leaders are separate full-sheet people only after materialization.

Persisted named institutions are stored in `owner:institutions_and_households`. Their homogeneous units remain the organizational owners during travel and combat; an operation changes assignment/formation, not unit identity. The institution cannot create personnel, equipment, money, mounts, or a leader merely because its name is mentioned.

A school monthly receipt covers attendance, instruction, facility availability, injury, qualification, recruitment, departure, fees, patronage, and development. An escort monthly receipt covers contracts, routes, crew assignment, travel, casualties, equipment, pay, reputation evidence/events, and development. Every crossed month is settled exactly once.




## Institutional persistence

A mercenary company owns units, treasury, payroll, food, ammunition, equipment, horses, contracts, losses, recruitment, training, headquarters or camp, and current employer. It is a reputation subject, but audience-specific reputation state lives only under `state/reputation/`. Companies do not appear from a generic random table after first contact; persisted companies in `state/merc/` and `state/force/` already exist and continue whether or not Tang Wei sees them.

## Tang defense doctrine

Current fortress-defense assignments, contracted-company duties, House force roles and retinue attachments are mutable state. Read them from current contracts, orders, formations and assignments. This rule defines only that assignments require real authority and do not transfer ownership unless a lawful transfer says so.

All 320 bed crossbows, 48 trebuchets, 128 stone-drop cranes, and 128 firepot cauldrons can be crewed at minimum simultaneously by the 7,500-person contracted establishment. Continuous multi-shift operation still requires relief allocation; one person cannot occupy incompatible duties at the same time.

## Contract ledger

Every contract records payer, company, start, expiry or renewal, monthly pay, advance, food, housing, equipment issue, casualty compensation, family compensation, loot policy, surrender authority, secrecy, allowed and forbidden areas, desertion consequences, command hierarchy, medical obligations, and termination. Loyalty changes only from supported pay, treatment, losses, leadership, alternatives, enemy offers, family safety, audience-known reputation, and survival expectations.

Tang weapons used by contractors remain House Tang property in fortress armories. Contractors receive no Tang horses, breeding stock, stud access, or production secrets.




Mountain populations are persistent world owners rather than decorative prose. Initial population and force seeds are stored in `owner:independent_mountain_units`. These values are game-balance inputs, not canon census claims.

Yotanwa and every other named mountain character remain separate full-sheet identities. Anonymous mountain warriors, hunters, scouts, skirmishers, craftspeople, and civilians remain units. Mixed civilians cannot enter combat without age, health, role, equipment, and authority resolution. Combat resolves directly from the actual units at their lawful current headcount.




The persisted world contains an emerging northern steppe confederation, independent pastoral clans, persistent raider networks, and autonomous fortified communities. They are not an eighth Warring State and are not called Mongols. Their military advantages are mounted mobility, moving archery, scouting, survival, route knowledge, dispersed remounts, and operational endurance. Their disadvantages are dense siege warfare, heavy infantry assault, centralized grain logistics, and long stationary campaigns.

Each owner settles population, herds, horses, pasture, weather, trade, tribute, raids, recruitment, losses, migration, diplomacy, and training. A raid transfers real stock and creates casualties, pursuit, resentment, and future scarcity. A clan does not respawn after defeat.


Audience-specific reputation/renown/prestige/notoriety lives only under `state/reputation/` and updates through delivered evidence; institution files do not own a universal reputation score.
