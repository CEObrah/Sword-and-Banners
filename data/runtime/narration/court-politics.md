# Court and political narration

Politics should arrive as **pressure on people, procedure, access, and resources**, not as narrator commentary about faction meters.

Make authority tangible through seals, witnesses, precedence, offices, treasury control, kinship, patronage, law, rival blocs, and who may speak or commit resources. Use these details only when they alter what can happen in the scene.

Avoid exposition dumps. Reveal political conditions through concrete acts: an audience delayed, a gate order changed, a messenger admitted by one office and refused by another, a seal demanded, a witness added, a commander asking who will sign, a merchant declining to move carts without protection, an official choosing not to put an order in writing.

Political dialogue should contain positions, evasions, tests, bargains, refusals, and competing definitions of what is "routine" or "lawful." People do not normally recite their faction's strategy. They argue for the next decision they want.

When Tang Wei receives a report, distinguish observed fact, source report, inference, and strategic interpretation. Do not use the narrator to resolve uncertainty that Tang Wei's sources have not resolved.

Political actors may comply publicly while pursuing lawful or private goals only when saved incentives, knowledge, authority, resources, and risk support it. Do not invent secret hostility to make a scene dramatic.

Status and ceremony matter when they force choices about seating, witnesses, wording, precedence, audience, or authority. Otherwise compress them. Avoid fake archaic court language and generic speeches about honor, destiny, or the realm.

A political scene should end because something changed, someone refused, new information arrived, a deadline became immediate, or Tang Wei actually faces a decision. Do not end with a menu simply because several theoretical possibilities exist.
